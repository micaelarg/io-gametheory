# Referee 2: attack your own conclusion

Before writing the answer, raise the **three most damaging** objections and answer each - or lower your confidence and say so. Pick from this list the ones that actually bite for this question; do not recite the list.

## Model objections
- **Wrong game.** Is this really simultaneous? Who actually moves first, and can they commit? Is it really one-shot, or repeated (which changes everything: collusion, reputation)?
- **Missing player.** Landlord, regulator, a third entrant, suppliers, platforms (delivery apps change location games), the consumer as a strategic player.
- **Missing action.** Price as well as location; quality; exit; merging; lobbying; vertical integration; capacity as a commitment.
- **Wrong information.** Does the rival know my cost? Does the bidder know the object's value? Asymmetric information flips many results.
- **Equilibrium selection.** With several equilibria, why this one? What would move the market to another?
- **Rationality / sophistication.** Do these players actually compute best responses? Small firms imitate; consider level-k or simple rules of thumb. Firms that survived long may behave "as if" optimal.
- **Partial equilibrium.** Does the decision move prices/wages/rents elsewhere? (Opening an anchor store raises local rents.)
- **Lucas critique / response.** The rival will react to your move; demand parameters estimated before the change may not hold after.

## Data objections
- **Measurement.** OSM incompleteness, scraped prices are list prices (not paid prices), census is old, daytime vs night population.
- **Selection.** Observed stores are survivors; observed bids are from bidders who showed up; observed negotiations are those that closed.
- **Endogeneity.** Stores locate where demand is high *and* where they expect weak rivals; correlation between presence and sales is not causal.
- **Calibration.** Parameters borrowed from elsewhere (β in Huff, elasticities) - how sensitive is the conclusion to them? Run the sweep.

## Conclusion objections
- **Robustness.** Does the ranking/recommendation survive plausible parameter ranges? If not, say what you would need to know.
- **Magnitude.** Is the effect large enough to matter relative to noise and to fixed costs?
- **External validity.** Does the case it was calibrated on resemble this one?
- **Ethics/legal.** Does the recommendation amount to collusion, predatory pricing, bid rigging, or misuse of scraped personal data? Flag it.

## Output format

```
Objection 1 - <one line>.  Answer: <why it does/doesn't change the conclusion>.
Objection 2 - ...
Objection 3 - ...
Confidence: high / medium / low - because <the assumption that matters most>.
```
